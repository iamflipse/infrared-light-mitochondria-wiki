When Light Meets Metabolism

The Big Picture

In one line: Roger Seheult argues that infrared photons from the sun change how often mitochondrial electron-transfer steps reach a favorable arrangement, and that the same light can speed one step and slow another.

This is his preprint with Fosbury, Zimmerman, and Jeffery. He says they have not generated the experimental data. The numbers below are his reports of other papers, his own light sensor, or a slide.

The argument in six steps

  1. The sun is rich in a particular infrared. He puts the overview peak near 0.74 electron volts, then a photosphere and an atmospheric window near 0.75 electron volts, about 1,500 nanometers. Visible light carries more energy. This band carries more photons. → The Infrared Window
  2. That light can go through a body. A University College London measurement, July of the previous year, detected infrared on the far side of a thorax, and through six clothing layers. Incident solar infrared scales as mass to the three-quarter power, which is also how metabolism scales. He calls that plausible, and he says it does not prove the claim. → Through the Body
  3. Older infrared stories do not explain a slowdown. A 2005 account has red or infrared light raising ATP at cytochrome c oxidase. Another has infrared loosening water around the ATP rotor. In a 2018 paper he cites, 810 nanometers increased complex IV activity and 750 and 950 nanometers decreased it. → Why Older Accounts Fall Short
  4. Marcus theory supplies the sign. Lambda is reorganization energy, not wavelength. If the crossing is already at the bottom, vibration slows the step. If the crossing is up the curve, vibration speeds it. Marcus predicted the inverted region in the 1980s. He says another group confirmed it in 1988. The Nobel Prize in chemistry was 1992. → Marcus Theory
  5. A broad spectrum is his preferred case. One wavelength may speed most steps and slow some. Sunlight, and a 60 watt incandescent bulb, spread the wavelengths. He still says monochromatic 850 and 940 nanometer ICU studies shortened stays by about 30 percent. → Broad Light and Narrow Light
  6. He names the assistance photometabolism. Not photosynthesis. The body is not making energy from light. Light is helping electron transfer. Flu, and several major causes of death, peak one to three weeks after the shortest day. → Photometabolism · Seasons and Latitude

What he tells people in the room

These are his words in the talk, not a protocol.

  • Go outside, including into shade. Cover up if you burn. His low-E dining room read 0.810 watts per square meter. Shade under trees was over 29, and he cites 41.9. → Shade, Glass, and Indoors
  • At high latitude the winter sun is not the obstacle. The schedule is. Plan the short daylight, for example at lunch. Infrared goes through clothes. Ultraviolet does not, very well.
  • Hospital roofs in Melbourne and London are his picture of high technology plus sunlight. He does not report a measured stay reduction from those roofs. → Hospitals and Heliotherapy

Read this before the details

  • The two eye studies are not head to head. He says so.
  • The 30 percent pharmacy and nursing sketch is his "if," applied to the roofs.
  • Pseudomonas biofilm, and infrared improving immunity, are studies he remembers. He says he does not have the immune study with him.
  • Even-numbered versus odd-numbered wavelengths is a questioner's suggestion. His answer is that nobody has mapped the steps yet.
  • 0.74 and 0.75 electron volts are both spoken. The shade figures "over 29" and 41.9 are both spoken.
