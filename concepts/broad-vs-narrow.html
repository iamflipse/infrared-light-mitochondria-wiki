When Light Meets Metabolism

Broad Light and Narrow Light

One wavelength can help most steps and hinder some. Sunlight, he thinks, covers more of the crossings. He does not dismiss the single-wavelength trials.

After the vent yeast work he sets three environments side by side. No infrared, which is the built environment. Monochromatic infrared. Broad-spectrum infrared. Monochromatic light, on the hypothesis, may speed most electron-transfer steps and slow some. He says randomized trials already show benefit from that. Broad spectrum is the case he calls best, because many wavelengths would lower more of the barriers. He marks it as a hypothesis.

The eye, two studies

Two papers in Nature Scientific Reports, same lab, on telling red from blue at the cones. He calls the retina probably the highest concentration of mitochondria in the body. Monochromatic 850 nanometer light: a 9 percent improvement in visual acuity in the red cones and 16 percent in the blue cones, as he states it.

Jeffery then uses a 60 watt incandescent bulb at a desk. Most of that energy, Seheult says, is invisible infrared, and it is broad. He reports about a 20 percent improvement, lasting for weeks.

"I will caution this was not a head-to-head trial."

"broad spectrum infrared may out perform monochromatic illumination because mitochondrial respiration is an ensemble of many distinct Marcus electron transfer reactions."

"May outperform" stays conditional. Two populations, two studies.

The ICU, one wavelength, and it still worked

A Brazil LED jacket at 940 nanometers, monochromatic: length of stay down by 30 percent. He says it works. A second Brazil study, all ICU patients, randomized and triple blinded, 850 nanometers: 30 percent shorter stay and increased strength. Again, monochromatic works.

"But I think if you want to have the edge, polychromatic may work better."

The spectra he shows next to this are an incandescent bulb, the solar spectrum, and an LED, plus low-E window glass. That comparison is the bridge to Shade, Glass, and Indoors.
