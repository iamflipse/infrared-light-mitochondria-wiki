# Glossary

Terms from the talk, in plain words. Each one is his usage, not a textbook rewrite.

- **Photometabolism.** Light assisting electron transfer. Not photosynthesis, and not the body making energy from the sun. See [Photometabolism](photometabolism.md).
- **Electron transport chain.** The path he calls rate-limiting for ATP. Electrons move between complexes and finish on oxygen at complex four.
- **Cytochrome c oxidase (complex IV, COX).** The last complex. The 2005 account puts the infrared effect here. See [Why Older Accounts Fall Short](older-models.md).
- **Delta G.** Gibbs free energy of a redox step. The look-up value for how much energy can pump protons. Not the same as reorganization energy.
- **Lambda.** Reorganization energy: the twist of the protein and the reordering of the solvent before an electron moves. He says this lambda is not wavelength.
- **Activationless transfer.** The crossing is already at the bottom of the reactant curve. Added infrared slows it.
- **Inverted region.** A reaction so downhill that the rate falls and energy has to be put back in. Predicted in the 1980s, confirmed, he says, in 1988. Nobel in 1992.
- **0.75 electron volts.** The infrared photon energy he says the solar photosphere over-produces and the atmosphere admits, about 1,500 nanometers. He also says 0.74 on the overview.
- **Monochromatic.** One wavelength, as in the 850 and 940 nanometer studies.
- **Polychromatic, or broad spectrum.** Many wavelengths at once. Sunlight, or the incandescent bulb in the eye study.
- **Low-E glass.** The coating on his dining-room windows. The sensor read 0.810 watts per square meter there.
- **Photobiomodulation.** The host's name for the science of light used on biology. Seheult's own coined word in the talk is photometabolism.
- **MRPS18.** A protein he says rose about 6.7 fold in the infrared yeast work.
- **Biofilm.** The layer he says Pseudomonas uses to keep antibiotics out. He recalls a study in which infrared switched that production off.
- **Heliotherapy.** Historical medical use of sunlight. He answers with Rollier, Kellogg, penicillin, and a 1942 hospital plan.
