# Yeast at the Vents

**Infrared increased yeast growth where the sun does not reach, and the increase was not explained by glucose.**

He turns to hydrothermal vents. Volcanic activity, a lot of infrared, and yeast, which are eukaryotes with mitochondria. With infrared, growth went up sharply. Glucose availability did not account for it. Infrared, in an actual organism and not only a model, was controlling metabolism, in his reading.

Temperature still mattered. Colder temperatures slowed growth. More infrared could offset the cold. He compares dark, 880 nanometers, and 940 nanometers.

> "The activation energy for growth was calculated on average at 12 K cals per mole."

> "you could dramatically reduce activation energy for growth from 12 K cals down to 2 K cals."

He does not put the whole effect on the electron transport chain. They think protein synthesis is involved, because gentamicin, which affects protein synthesis, could mitigate the growth increase. Mitochondria in the exposed organisms had healthier shapes, as he reports it. The protein he names is MRPS18, up about 6.7 fold, associated with mitochondrial genes.

The three-way contrast he draws from this is on [Broad Light and Narrow Light](broad-vs-narrow.md): no infrared, one wavelength, or a broad spectrum. The vent result is his exhibit that infrared can change growth apart from food and, within limits, apart from how cold the water is.
