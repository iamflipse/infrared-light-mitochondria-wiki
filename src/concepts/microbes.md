# Microbes and Wavelength Maps

**He says the same electron-transfer idea could apply to bacteria. He does not adopt an even-versus-odd wavelength rule.**

A questioner asks about yeast overgrowth, which is a different yeast than the vent organism, and about bacteria that have electron transport chains, including the microbiome. Seheult says the mechanism could apply.

He then says infrared has been shown to improve immune systems. He does not have that study with him.

The case he remembers is Pseudomonas. It can resist antibiotics. It makes biofilm that antibiotics do not cross. Weeks of a drug that works in a dish can be followed by relapse, because of the biofilm. A study he says has just come out found that infrared can switch off Pseudomonas biofilm production. He reads that as a possible help to antibiotics or to immunity, if you are in the sun with that kind of biofilm.

> "the the placement of infrared light can actually switch off the ability for Pseudomonas to produce biofilms,"

He grants the other half. Infrared might help the mitochondria of bacteria, good and bad. The immune system, he says, is helped in other ways that could offset that. Both halves are his speculation in the answer, and the biofilm paper is one he is remembering rather than showing.

## The map he says nobody has made

The next questioner proposes a pattern: even numbers in the infrared more excitatory, the 700s and 900s more inhibitory. That pattern is the question. Seheult does not state it as a finding.

His program of work:

- Isolate each transfer. Four complexes, and a lot more reactions than four.
- Look at the proteins around them and the reorganization energies. The caption says "organization energies."
- Sweep wavelength and see which steps speed and which slow.

An activationless step slows if you add vibration. A high barrier speeds up. The two parabolas can answer in opposite directions to the same light.

> "No one's done that yet, but that's something that we should definitely do."

That is the same Marcus picture as [Marcus Theory](marcus.md), stated as unfinished lab work rather than as a result.
