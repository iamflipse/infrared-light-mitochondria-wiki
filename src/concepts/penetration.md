# Through the Body

**Longer wavelengths pass through tissue, clothes, and a hospital blanket. He says the amount of solar infrared scales like metabolism.**

A paper he dates to July of the previous year, Glenn Jeffery and Bob Fosbury at University College London, detected infrared coming out the other side of a thorax. Much weaker, but there. The same study, as he shows it, sent light through three articles of clothing, six layers, on an infrared camera. He says many people already accept that infrared penetrates the skull. The thorax result is for people who doubt the torso.

> "they could actually detect infrared light coming the other side of a thorax, albeit much more diminished."

> "clothes actually do not block much of the infrared light"

He then puts incident infrared solar power against body mass. Fly, dog, human, elephant. It scales as mass to the three-quarter power, including light that only hits one side and has to get through tissue. Metabolism scales the same way. A fruit fly, a mouse, and a human fall on that line in watts, as he describes the graph.

> "It doesn't prove it, but it's plausible that sunlight could actually affect and regulate metabolism because it scales as does metabolism."

That is an argument from matching exponents. It is not an experiment of theirs.

Later, on the King's College roof, he says the infrared is going through the blanket and the gown into the body. In the high-latitude answer he separates the two bands: ultraviolet does not penetrate clothes very well, and infrared does.

The practical reading he wants is on [Shade, Glass, and Indoors](built-environment.md): you do not have to stand in the direct sun for the infrared he is talking about.
