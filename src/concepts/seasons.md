# Seasons and Latitude

**Flu, and several major causes of death, peak one to three weeks after the shortest day. He reads that as the sun modulating metabolism.**

He moves quickly. COVID surges in Europe, he says, tracked latitude more than humidity and temperature. Ultraviolet from the sun was associated with lower mortality in multiple studies, as he reports it. With Margaret Scutch in Mexico, in countries where 50 percent of the population was overweight, latitude correlated with COVID-19 mortality. The name is the caption's spelling.

Influenza peaks almost exactly every year, one to three weeks after the shortest day. Australia follows the same rule, and its shortest day is in June. Christmas and New Year parties therefore fall in the low-flu season. He says parties may play a part, and that they are not the primary reason.

> "Singapore, which is literally just 80 miles from the equator, they have no pattern of influenza"

The sun never leaves long enough, in his phrase.

In the United States, deaths from heart disease, cancer, stroke, chronic lower respiratory disease, and Alzheimer's, infectious and non-infectious together, rise and fall together.

> "they all go up together, they all come down together."

Peak one to three weeks after the shortest day. Nadir one to three weeks after the longest day. If what mitochondria want is many wavelengths at once, then this is the sun timing metabolism. That is the step into the word [photometabolism](photometabolism.md).

Latitude comes back in the question period, for blood pressure and multiple sclerosis. He treats those as real geographic patterns. The blood-pressure one, before modern drugs, he explains with ultraviolet and nitric oxide in the skin. The melting snow at high latitude is his infrared example.
