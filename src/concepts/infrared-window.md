# The Infrared Window

**Most of the sun's energy is in the visible. Most of the photons are in a small infrared band.**

He uses a money-from-heaven picture. The most common bill is not the pile worth the most. Visible wavelengths carry the most energy. The most photons sit near 0.75 electron volts, about 1,500 nanometers, more than a plain blackbody spectrum would put there. On the overview slide he had said a peak around 0.74 electron volts. Both figures are his. This page does not collapse them.

> "the most amount of energy is coming in the visible spectrum, but the most amount of photons that come from the sun are actually here in this little band."

He sketches why. A photosphere of hydrogen around the sun favors photons around that energy. The atmosphere has deep cuts in the infrared where molecules absorb, and a window where this band gets through. His phrase is that the sun and the atmosphere are tuned to let those photons in.

> "we have a sun and an atmosphere that is perfectly tuned to allow more infrared photons in around that wavelength of 0.75 electron volts and 1,500 nanometers."

The hypothesis that follows: sunlight may be especially effective because, unlike one wavelength, it contains a broad spectrum that can interact with many vibrational modes and many electron-transfer steps.

He is not claiming the group measured the solar spectrum. This is the astrophysics he says Fosbury brought, which he crashed a course in, with a chemistry and biology degree from UC Riverside behind him.
