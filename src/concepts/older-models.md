# Why Older Accounts Fall Short

**He does not throw out the older papers. He says they cannot explain wavelengths that slow the chain.**

Foods, sugars, fatty acids, and amino acids enter the TCA cycle. Reducing agents feed the electron transport chain in the matrix. He calls that chain the rate-limiting step for ATP. Electrons move from complex to complex. Complex four is cytochrome c oxidase. Four electrons go onto oxygen, water is made, protons are pumped into the intermembrane space, and they return through ATP synthase. That is the currency, in his phrase.

Two accounts of why red or infrared light would speed this up:

1. A 2005 paper: the light acts on cytochrome c oxidase, which has a receptor for a particular wavelength, and cellular ATP goes up. He says this set the stage, and that it is too simple.
2. Water around the ATP rotor. Infrared structures that water, viscosity falls, the motor spins faster, and the rate-limiting step loosens.

> "I am not saying that these papers are incorrect or that they don't contribute."

The observation he says they do not cover is from 2018. At 810 nanometers, complex IV activity increased. At 750 nanometers, it decreased. At 950 nanometers, it decreased. 750 and 950 reduced purified COX activity by about 5 to 6 percent each. In intact mitochondria those wavelengths reduced oxygen consumption by 24 to 25 percent.

> "infrared light can be not only excitatory in the mitochondria, but also inhibitory."

He adds that some groups use infrared to reduce ATP production in neurons, to ask whether that helps in stroke or ischemia. The sign can be down on purpose. A mechanism that only speeds cytochrome c oxidase, or only loosens the rotor, does not tell you when the sign flips.

That is why he turns to [Marcus Theory](marcus.md).

Rudolph Marcus, he says, died this year at 102, did the work at Caltech, and won the Nobel Prize in chemistry in 1992 for how electrons transfer.
